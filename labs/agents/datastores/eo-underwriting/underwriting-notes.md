# E&O underwriting notes

*Team-authored working notes for the ClearFrame grounding corpus. Original prose
summarising publicly documented E&O application practice. Not legal advice, and
not a substitute for a carrier's underwriting guidelines.*

## Why the report exists

Errors & Omissions insurance is a delivery requirement: distributors, streamers,
and broadcasters will not accept a title without a policy, and underwriters will
not issue one without a credible clearance report. The report is therefore not
paperwork after the work — it is the gate between a finished picture and any
revenue.

## What an underwriter looks for

1. **Completeness.** An itemised register of every third-party element, anchored
   to where it appears. Gaps read as an incomplete search, and an incomplete
   search is what an underwriter prices for.
2. **Chain of title.** For each non-trivial item, who controls it *today*, traced
   through any transfers, with sources.
3. **Resolution.** For each exposure: licensed, altered, replaced, removed, or
   documented as defensible, with the document attached.
4. **Live sources.** Assertions that cannot be traced to a source are treated as
   unsupported.
5. **Named human sign-off.** A report is signed by a person who takes
   responsibility for it. Automated research supports that person; it does not
   replace them.

## Common exclusions and what triggers them

Carriers commonly exclude or condition coverage where the application reveals:

- music used without both chains licensed;
- artwork or footage whose provenance is unestablished;
- a depicted person who has objected, or a portrayal capable of a defamatory
  reading;
- an item disclosed as a known dispute at the time of application;
- fair use relied on without a written opinion from counsel.

The pattern is consistent: **a disclosed and resolved exposure is insurable; an
undisclosed one is a coverage dispute.** Surfacing a problem is worth more than
appearing to have none.

## Risk banding used in this system

- **GREEN** — no action required, or genuinely de minimis on the terms above. The
  chain of title is established and nothing encumbers it.
- **AMBER** — licensable or alterable. The production has a choice with a price.
  Includes any item whose chain of title could not be fully established.
- **RED** — blocking exposure. Litigation or an active dispute touching the item;
  an unresolved chain on a hero use; a living artist's work used without licence;
  a depicted person with a live objection.

**Any litigation signal is RED.** A suit or dispute touching the chain of title is
the fact pattern underwriters treat as uninsurable until resolved, and it is not
subject to balancing against prominence or cost.

## Fair use in an underwriting context

Fair use is a defence, not a clearance, and it is assessed after a claim is made.
Underwriters generally require a written opinion from qualified counsel before
accepting reliance on it — commonly for documentary and news uses. A system like
this one may draft the memo's factual predicate; the opinion belongs to a human.

## Continuous exposure

A clearance report is accurate on the day it is signed. Catalog acquisitions,
newly filed suits, lapsed registrations, and archive corrections all change the
picture afterwards, and the change is invisible until it becomes a claim. A watch
armed on each non-green rights holder, with a documented reopen protocol, is the
difference between a photograph and a live position.
