REEL 01Executive summary — the one-sheet

What it is. ClearFrame is an autonomous rights-clearance system for film and television. A producer uploads a script or a cut; a crew of specialized AI agents extracts every clearable element (music, lyrics, brands, artwork, real-person likenesses, archival footage, locations, fonts), investigates each one across the live web with citation-backed research, adversarially verifies its own findings, scores legal exposure on a green/amber/red taxonomy, drafts license outreach for human approval, and renders an E&O-ready clearance report in which every claim links to a live source. When the cut changes, only the changed items are re-investigated. After clearance ships, monitors keep watching the web — a lawsuit filed against a rights holder three weeks later reopens the item automatically.

Why it wins here. It is built precisely on the hackathon's mandated spine — Gemini Enterprise Agent Builder orchestrating a multi-agent ensemble on Google Cloud — and it is the strongest possible showcase of the Parallel partner track, because the product is impossible without live, cited web research: remove Parallel and no product exists. It occupies a category no other team will touch (content operations and legal infrastructure, not content generation), its autonomy is real and judge-testable on arbitrary input, and its demo contains two beats no simulated project can match: agents arguing over live evidence, and a cleared item reopening itself weeks later because the world changed.

The one-sentence memory hook — the thing a judge repeats at dinner after 150 demos: "the agents found the lawsuit hiding in scene 42."

REEL 02The problem: clearance, chain of title, E&O

Before any film, series, or documentary can be distributed, every third-party element that appears in it must be cleared — its rights status established, and either licensed, altered, replaced, or defensibly justified. The scope is wider than most people outside the industry realize: every needle-drop and every hummed melody; every visible brand, poster, painting, tattoo, and logo; every real person depicted or named; every second of archival or stock footage; typefaces; product packaging; even the artwork hanging out of focus in the background of a dinner scene. A 110-page feature script routinely yields two to four hundred clearable items.

Music is the canonical trap, because a single song is two properties with separate chains of title: the master recording (the specific recorded performance, usually controlled by a label) and the underlying composition (the written work, controlled by publishers, songwriters, or their estates). Licensing one without the other clears nothing. Compositions change hands through catalog acquisitions; estates fracture; publishers of record go stale. Establishing who controls what, today, is genuine investigative work across performing-rights databases, copyright registries, trade press, court records, and corporate filings — the live web, not a static dataset.

The commercial gate that makes this non-optional is Errors & Omissions (E&O) insurance. Distributors, streamers, and broadcasters will not accept delivery of a title without an E&O policy, and underwriters will not issue one without a credible clearance report — an itemized account of what appears in the film, who owns it, and how each exposure was resolved. Clearance is therefore not an efficiency problem; it is a structural gate between a finished film and any revenue at all.

How the work is done today

A clearance coordinator breaks down the script by hand into a spreadsheet. Paralegals and clearance-house researchers then grind through PRO databases, copyright and trademark registries, stock-agency records, news archives, and phone calls — item by item, for two to eight weeks, at costs that run from tens of thousands of dollars on an indie to well into six figures on a studio feature with a music-heavy cut. The output is a static document that is stale the moment the picture is re-edited, and blind the moment the world changes: a catalog acquisition or a newly filed lawsuit after sign-off is invisible until it becomes a claim. Documentaries suffer worst — archival-heavy, fair-use-dependent, and budget-poor — and clearance is a common reason finished documentaries never reach an audience.

Why the failure modes are expensive

The industry's cautionary tales are all the same shape: a song licensed on the master but not the composition; background artwork that turned out to be a living artist's work; a trademark owner who objected after the trailer shipped; archival footage whose provenance dissolved under scrutiny. The consequences range from forced re-edits and pulled festival slots to injunctions and settlements — and every one of them was, in principle, discoverable in advance by someone with enough hours to look. ClearFrame's thesis is that "enough hours to look" is now an API call.

REEL 03Landscape & the white space

The adjacent products that exist today validate the market and leave the category open. MovieChainer is a system of record for rights you already hold: contract lifecycle, royalty waterfalls, distribution deals, library administration. It assumes the rights facts are known and signed, then keeps them organized — it does not investigate the open web to establish who owns the cover song in scene 42. Flawless is an AI editing suite (visual dubbing, ADR, performance transfer, localization) whose "clear rights and clean provenance" positioning concerns its own generated outputs being ring-fenced and copyrightable — provenance of AI generation, not clearance of third-party elements in your footage. Clearance houses remain human-hours consultancies with no software moat. Point tools (cue-sheet managers) file paperwork but do not investigate.

The strongest single piece of evidence for the gap: when Google's own AI search mode is asked directly whether a platform exists for tracking chain of title and clearance, the best it can produce is a contracts database and an editing tool. When the world's best search engine cannot name a product in the category, that is not competition — it is documented white space, and it belongs in the demo video as a ten-second beat.

REEL 04Vision, principles & core concepts

Vision. Every frame cleared, every right traced, before the lawyers open the file — and kept cleared after they close it. ClearFrame turns clearance from a slow, one-shot document into a fast, living system: an autonomous investigation that a producer starts with an upload and a counsel finishes with an approval.

Operating principles

1 · Gemini is the brain, Parallel is the senses. Gemini (via Vertex AI, orchestrated by Agent Builder) does all reasoning, planning, extraction, risk judgment, and drafting. Parallel does all perception of the outside world: retrieval, page extraction, structured deep research, entity discovery, and continuous monitoring. Neither substitutes for the other, which is what keeps both judging panels — Google's and Parallel's — fully satisfied.

2 · No claim without a citation. Every factual assertion an agent makes about ownership, status, or litigation must carry at least one live-web citation with a stored snapshot. Findings without citations cannot advance past the Verifier. This is simultaneously the product's legal credibility, its anti-hallucination architecture, and its demo authenticity.

3 · Agents are supervised — by other agents and by humans. The Verifier adversarially re-checks researchers; counsel gates every red item and every outbound message. Autonomy is spent on investigation, never on legal judgment or external communication.

4 · Research is drafted cheap and escalated expensive. The Dispatcher assigns Parallel processor tiers by risk and confidence — fast recon for everything, deep dossiers only where exposure justifies cost — with a visible per-project budget meter.

5 · The ledger is append-only and the report is a view. Nothing is ever edited in place; every finding, challenge, decision, and approval appends to an immutable ledger, and the E&O report is rendered from it. Audit is a property, not a feature.

6 · Clearance is continuous. A cleared item is an armed monitor, not a closed row. The world changes; ClearFrame notices.

The journey, upload to armed watch

A producer drags in a cut (or a script PDF, pre-production). The Breakdown agent extracts items with timecodes into the register; the 1st AD plans the pass, budgets it, and fans investigations out to the specialist crews over Pub/Sub. Findings stream back with citations; the Verifier challenges anything weak; Risk Counsel scores what survives. Red and amber items queue mitigations at the human gate. On approval, the Outreach agent's license inquiries move to the send queue, the Ledger agent appends everything, and the E&O report renders on demand. Shipping the report arms monitors on every rights holder that mattered — closing the loop that Reel 11 details.

REEL 06The agent crew

Eight distinct roles; the researcher role instantiates four specialists, and researcher instances fan out per item, so a pass may run dozens of concurrent agents. Each role is an Agent Builder playbook with a narrow toolbelt, explicit guardrails, and a Hollywood credit — the vocabulary the UI and the demo speak.

REEL 07System architecture on Google Cloud

The system is four planes stacked on an asynchronous spine. The experience plane is the war room web app, serving the four roles and subscribing to Firestore so every agent action streams to screen the moment it happens. The reasoning plane is Gemini Enterprise Agent Builder: each crew role is a playbook with guardrails, grounded on data stores loaded with E&O underwriting guidelines and clearance handbooks, calling Gemini on Vertex AI. The tool plane is a set of small Cloud Run services — the Parallel adapter, the report renderer, the ledger API, the webhook receiver — each holding exactly the credentials it needs via Secret Manager. The data plane is Firestore for live state, BigQuery for the permanent ledger and rights-holder graph, Cloud Storage for cuts and rendered reports. Pub/Sub carries everything between planes: investigation fan-out, verifier challenges, approval events, and inbound Monitor webhooks, which is what lets a pass survive a closed laptop and lets the Sentinel wake the system weeks later.

Interaction chaining — provenance as a first-class feature

Two mechanisms make the crew feel like one investigation instead of many API calls. When the Verifier challenges a finding, the follow-up research run passes the original run's interaction ID as previous_interaction_id, so the challenge inherits the full prior context — the re-run answers the objection rather than starting over. And when a Monitor webhook fires months later, the Sentinel launches a follow-up Task the same way, chaining the event's ID so the reopened investigation carries its entire history. In the ledger, every finding therefore has an unbroken provenance chain from first recon to final re-verification — which is precisely the property an E&O underwriter wants and precisely the Parallel feature almost no other team will know exists.

FindAll — building the rights-holder graph

When findings name a controlling entity, Outreach runs FindAll with explicit match conditions ("subsidiaries or catalog vehicles of publisher X"; "licensing contacts for archive Y") with auto-enrichments attached, so every match returns structured contacts, corporate parents, and licensing-desk details straight into the rights_holders graph. Across productions the graph compounds: the tenth film clears faster than the first because the system already knows half the counterparties.

Access pattern

Search and Extract flow through Parallel's managed MCP server, registered as an Agent Builder tool — the brief's "managed protocol adapters" phrase, satisfied literally. Task, Deep Research, FindAll, and Monitor flow through the Cloud Run adapter service, which holds the API key in Secret Manager, applies the tier policy, and normalizes results onto Pub/Sub. Agents never touch credentials; the adapter never reasons.

REEL 09Data model & the clearance ledger

Two stores with a strict division of labor. Firestore holds the living present: the current pass, item statuses, the activity feed the UI streams. BigQuery holds the permanent past: every finding, citation, challenge, decision, and cost event, append-only, plus the rights-holder graph. The E&O report is a rendered view over BigQuery; delete nothing, derive everything.

BigQuery, abridged DDL

-- the spine of auditability: append-only, hash-chained

);

  finding_id STRING, item_id STRING, agent STRING,

CREATE TABLE clearframe.citations (
  citation_id STRING, finding_id STRING,
  url STRING, snapshot_uri STRING, fetched_ts TIMESTAMP, authority STRING
);

Firestore mirrors only what the UI needs at interactive latency — passes/{id} with counters and budget state, items/{id} with status and risk, and a capped feed collection of the last few hundred events — everything else is a BigQuery query. Citation snapshots (rendered page captures at fetch time) live in Cloud Storage so the report's evidence survives link rot; the ledger's hash chain means any tampering with history is detectable by re-computation. None of this is exotic engineering, and that is the point: auditability as schema, not as promise.

REEL 10Investigation lifecycle & delta re-clearance

Every item walks the same state machine, and the state machine is the product's honesty: nothing reaches resolved without surviving verification, nothing red resolves without a human, and nothing resolved is ever final — it is monitored.

Delta re-clearance

Every item carries a content_hash derived from its extracted description and its scene/timecode anchor. When a new cut uploads, the Breakdown agent re-extracts, the Ledger agent diffs against the previous cut's item set, and the 1st AD queues only the difference: new items enter at queued, changed items re-enter at recon, removed items are marked withdrawn, and untouched items keep their state and their citations. A 214-item picture whose new cut changes 9 items re-clears in the time those 9 take — the ledger records the pass as a delta, and the E&O report gains a delta annex listing exactly what changed and what was re-verified. This is the moment enterprise judges lean forward, because it is the difference between a demo tool and a system a post-production schedule could actually live on: pictures get re-cut weekly, and clearance that restarts from zero each time is clearance nobody runs twice.

REEL 11Continuous clearance: the Sentinel loop

A clearance report is a photograph of the world on the day it was signed; the world keeps moving. Catalogs get acquired, estates go to court, trademark registrations lapse or change hands, archives issue retroactive corrections. ClearFrame's answer is the Sentinel: shipping a report arms a Parallel Monitor on every rights holder attached to a non-green item, watching for the specific change classes that would alter its risk state. REEL 13Security, governance & cost control

The uploaded asset is pre-release IP — the thing studios are most paranoid about leaking — so the security story is not compliance theater, it is a selling point. Everything stays inside the project's Google Cloud perimeter: cuts and scripts in Cloud Storage under project-scoped access, model calls through Vertex AI, no third-party model traffic. What leaves the perimeter is only what must: search objectives and item descriptions to Parallel, never the footage, never the script text beyond the item being investigated.

REEL 17How this wins the hackathon

The track arbitrage

All five prize buckets pay identically, so track choice is pure competition math. Replit will drown in vibe-coded submissions; ClickHouse and Grafana draw their data and ops crowds; IBM's story sits awkwardly beside a Gemini mandate. Parallel is the least-known partner in the lineup — the thinnest field — and simultaneously the deepest possible fit: a web-research platform built for agents, judged at an agent hackathon, shown a product that cannot exist without it. ClearFrame competes only inside that track, against a field that will mostly submit generic research assistants.

The five levers judges actually pull

Essentiality. Parallel's judges will ask one question of every project: did they need us? ClearFrame's answer is structural — hallucinated rights data is fatal in a legal context, so live cited research isn't an enhancement, it is the existence condition. No other viable answer in the track can say "remove the partner and there is no product."

Authenticity. Judges test the hosted URL, and ClearFrame welcomes it: upload anything, and the research that follows is real, on the real web, with citations a judge can click. After a day of staged autonomy, verifiable autonomy is what survives to morning. The brief's own words — agents "interacting with live web services" — are ClearFrame's default behavior, not its demo script.

The mandated shape, satisfied literally. Multi-step tool calls, database updates, triggered cloud jobs, sub-agents handing off state, managed protocol adapters, guardrails, token efficiency, user boundaries — every phrase in "What to Build" maps to a named component in Reels 06–13, and the submission write-up should make that mapping explicit, line by line.

Two unfakeable beats. The Verifier's challenge (agents arguing over live evidence, resolving differently on re-run) and the Sentinel's reopen (the system acting weeks after the humans left) are moments simulation cannot counterfeit — and they are ClearFrame's, structurally, because its substrate is the live web.

Completion probability. The scored deliverable is what ships by September 8. The Must tier of Reel 15 is a winning submission on its own; everything above it compounds the score. A 95%-finished ClearFrame beats a 70%-finished anything.

REEL 18After the credits: the company

Beyond the hackathon, ClearFrame is rights infrastructure for the content economy. The wedge is per-title clearance for documentaries and indies — desperate, underserved, fast to close — expanding upward to streamers' slates. Three moats compound with every pass: the rights-holder graph (a proprietary, continuously verified map of who controls what, which no one has assembled), outcome data (which flagged risks became claims, turning risk scoring into underwriting-grade signal), and the insurer channel (once E&O underwriters accept ClearFrame reports and price against its scores, the product sits inside the money flow — platform fees on premiums and on licenses transacted through Outreach). Expansion runs from continuous clearance for UGC platforms and brand-integration verification to the endgame market: rights clearance for AI training data and provenance certification for generated content, where every media company on earth is about to need exactly this machine.
